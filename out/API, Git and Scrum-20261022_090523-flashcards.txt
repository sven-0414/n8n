What is the main idea behind the Agile methodology?;Agile emphasizes small, iterative steps with fast feedback cycles, delivering working software in short sprints rather than building everything at once.

How does Agile differ from the Waterfall method?;Waterfall follows a sequential approach where phases are completed one after another, while Agile uses iterative cycles with frequent feedback and adaptability to changing requirements.

What are the four core values of the Agile Manifesto?;The four values are individuals and interactions over processes and tools, working software over comprehensive documentation, customer collaboration over contract negotiation, and responding to change over following a plan.

What is the purpose of a Sprint in Scrum?;A Sprint is a fixed-length development cycle (typically 2 weeks) during which a team delivers a potentially shippable product increment.

Who are the three roles in Scrum and what do they do?;The Product Owner defines what to build, the Scrum Master ensures the process works and removes obstacles, and Developers build the product and decide how to accomplish tasks.

What is the role of the Product Owner in Scrum?;The Product Owner decides what to build, talks to customers, owns the product backlog, and prioritizes items based on business value.

What are the five events in Scrum?;The five events are Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective, and the Sprint itself.

What is a User Story in Agile?;A User Story is a simple description of a feature written from the user’s perspective using the format: "As a [user], I want [functionality] so that [reason]."

What is the purpose of Story Points in Agile estimation?;Story Points are a relative sizing method used to estimate effort rather than time, helping teams compare work size and track velocity.

What is the main goal of the Daily Scrum?;The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute meeting where team members synchronize their work toward the sprint goal by discussing progress, plans, and blockers.

What are the three main artifacts in Scrum?;The three artifacts are the Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, and Increment (working product).

How does Kanban differ from Scrum in terms of rhythm and planning?;Kanban uses continuous flow without fixed sprints, while Scrum follows a structured rhythm with regular sprints and planning meetings.

What is the main advantage of using WIP limits in Kanban?;WIP limits help manage flow by preventing too many tasks from being worked on simultaneously, which improves focus and reduces delays.

What are some common mistakes made when implementing Agile?;Common mistakes include treating Daily Scrums as status reports to managers, skipping retrospectives, having an unavailable Product Owner, and pushing new work into ongoing sprints.

Which tool is most commonly used for Agile project management in enterprises?;Jira is the most widely used enterprise tool for managing Agile projects.

What does the term "velocity" mean in Scrum?;Velocity measures how many story points a team typically completes in a sprint, helping with future planning and tracking performance.

How should a team approach backlog refinement?;Backlog refinement involves reviewing items near the top, splitting large items into smaller ones, adding details and acceptance criteria, and estimating when appropriate.

What is the main benefit of using the Scrum framework?;Scrum provides structure for teams to work iteratively with regular feedback, continuous improvement, and predictable delivery cycles.

What does the term "Definition of Done" refer to in Agile?;The Definition of Done is a set of criteria that must be met before a product increment can be considered complete and potentially shippable.

Why is it important to keep Sprint Goals consistent during a sprint?;Consistent Sprint Goals provide focus for the team, prevent scope creep, and help ensure alignment on what needs to be delivered.

What is an Epic in Agile?;An Epic is a large user story that is too big to complete in one sprint and must be broken down into smaller stories.

Why should teams avoid "Scrum-but" syndrome?;Teams should avoid "Scrum-but" because it means doing Scrum without key elements like regular reviews, retrospectives, or feedback mechanisms, which undermines the method’s effectiveness.

What is the main idea behind the Kanban method?;Kanban focuses on visualizing work, limiting Work in Progress, and managing continuous flow to improve efficiency and reduce bottlenecks.

How do teams estimate story sizes in Agile?;Teams use techniques like Planning Poker or story points to estimate relative effort, often using a Fibonacci sequence to compare work size.

What is the benefit of regular retrospectives in Scrum?;Retrospectives help teams reflect on their processes, identify areas for improvement, and implement changes that enhance future performance.

What is the difference between a Product Backlog and a Sprint Backlog?;The Product Backlog contains all potential features or requirements prioritized by value, while the Sprint Backlog includes only those items selected for the current sprint along with a plan to complete them.

Why is customer collaboration important in Agile?;Customer collaboration ensures that teams understand user needs and can adapt quickly to feedback, leading to more relevant and valuable products.

How does continuous improvement work in Scrum?;Continuous improvement occurs through regular retrospectives where teams discuss what went well, what didn’t, and how they can improve their process.
